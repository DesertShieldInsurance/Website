What General Liability Covers for a Trucking Business

Your auto liability policy covers incidents involving the truck on the road. General liability covers everything else — a visitor injured at your yard, damage caused loading or unloading at a customer's dock, or a products/completed operations claim tied to your business operations.

It's not a coverage FMCSA requires the way it requires auto liability, but nearly every broker, shipper, and warehouse now requires proof of general liability before they'll let a driver on the property — often through a certificate of insurance naming them as an additional insured.

Typical Limits and What Drives Them

$1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate is the standard limit most contracts and lease agreements ask for. Fleets with more customer-facing exposure, or those that lease space at multiple terminals, sometimes carry higher limits or layer excess coverage on top.

Premium is driven by your operations, payroll, number of trucks, and whether you have any warehousing, cross-docking, or loading/unloading exposure beyond just point-to-point hauling.

Common Situations General Liability Responds To

  • A third party injured while on your lot or at your dock
  • Property damage caused during loading or unloading (outside the vehicle itself)
  • A products or completed-operations claim tied to freight you handled
  • Advertising injury or a slip-and-fall at your place of business

General liability is not a coverage FMCSA requires by federal regulation; it is commonly required contractually by brokers, shippers, and facility owners.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need general liability if I only drive, and don't have a yard?

Many owner-operators still carry it because brokers and shippers now require a GL certificate as a condition of doing business, even without a fixed facility.

Does general liability cover cargo or auto accidents?

No. Cargo and auto liability are separate policies. General liability specifically excludes automobile-related claims, which is why trucking businesses carry all three together.

Can general liability and auto liability be bundled?

Sometimes, depending on the carrier and your operation, but they're always distinct coverage parts even when packaged together on one policy.
