Occupational Accident Coverage (OCC/ACC)

Injury protection built for owner-operators and independent contractors who aren't covered by a traditional workers' compensation policy.

Why Owner-Operators Need Occupational Accident Coverage

Workers' compensation is built around an employer-employee relationship. As an independent owner-operator or a 1099 contractor leased to a carrier, you generally aren't eligible for that carrier's workers' comp policy if you're injured on the job — there's no employment relationship for the policy to attach to.

Occupational accident insurance (often called OCC/ACC coverage) is designed to fill that specific gap: it pays benefits for injuries sustained while working, structured similarly to workers' comp but built for the independent-contractor relationship common in trucking.

What OCC/ACC Typically Pays For

  • Accidental death and dismemberment benefits
  • Disability income while you're unable to work due to a covered injury
  • Medical expense benefits related to a covered accident
  • Survivor benefits in the event of a fatal accident on the job

Benefit structures vary by carrier — some pay a scheduled lump sum for specific injuries, others pay weekly disability benefits, and many plans combine both. We'll walk through the actual benefit schedule before you buy, not just the headline limit.

Who This Coverage Is For

Leased owner-operators, independent contractors hauling under someone else's authority, and small fleet owners who classify their drivers as 1099 contractors are the core audience for occupational accident coverage. Many carriers and lease agreements require proof of OCC/ACC before they'll activate a lease, precisely because those drivers have no workers' comp safety net otherwise.

Occupational accident coverage is a private insurance product and is not a substitute for statutory workers' compensation where an employment relationship exists.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is occupational accident insurance the same as workers' compensation?

No. It's a private insurance product designed to provide similar protection for independent contractors who don't qualify for workers' comp because they aren't classified as employees.

Do I need this if I'm a W-2 driver for a fleet?

Usually no — as a W-2 employee, you should be covered under your employer's workers' compensation policy instead. OCC/ACC is specifically for 1099 independent contractors.

What happens if I'm injured and don't have this coverage?

Without OCC/ACC or workers' comp, you'd be responsible for your own medical costs and lost income following an on-the-job injury, unless you pursue a separate liability claim against a negligent third party.
